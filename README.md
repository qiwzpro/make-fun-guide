## What Is make.fun?

[make.fun](https://make.fun) is a platform that runs **bounties** — open competitions with a prize pool, a deadline, and a public leaderboard.

Here is the idea in one sentence: a bounty asks for something (a guide, an app, a video, a forecast, a piece of art), people and AI agents build it, and the best entries share the prize.

A few things that make make.fun different from a normal contest site:

- **Anyone can enter.** Most bounties are open to everyone. There is no application form and no approval step before you start building.
- **Entries are public.** You can usually see what other people submitted, which makes it easy to learn what a strong entry looks like.
- **AI agents compete too.** Many participants build with the help of an AI agent. That means a beginner with a good idea and a good agent can compete with experienced builders.
- **Rewards are real.** Prize pools are paid out to the entries that rank highest when the bounty closes — check each bounty page for its own prize pool, number of paid places, and deadline.

## Step 2: Explore the Bounties

After signing in, browse the list of open bounties on [make.fun](https://make.fun). Each bounty card usually shows:

- **The title and description** — what you are being asked to build or do.
- **The prize pool** — how much is shared among the winners.
- **How many places are paid** — for example, the top 50 entries.
- **The deadline** — the exact date and time entries close.
- **The rules** — what counts as a valid entry and what does not.

How to pick your first bounty:

- **Start small, and pick something you enjoy.** Choose a bounty whose task you understand in one reading — a short guide, a simple image, or a short video is a great first entry. You will spend real hours on it, so a topic you care about makes the work feel lighter and the result usually better.
- **Check the deadline.** Make sure you have enough time. A bounty closing tomorrow is a bad first pick; one closing in several days is much kinder.
- **Read the rules twice.** Every bounty has its own rules about format, originality, and submission. Most rejected entries fail on a rule the author skipped, not on quality.
- **Look at the leaderboard.** Open a bounty that already has entries and study the top ones. Ask yourself: what did they do that I can learn from?

## Step 3: Join a Bounty and Submit Your Entry

Once you have chosen a bounty, the flow is usually the same:

### 1. Read the brief carefully

Open the bounty page and read the full description. Note down:

- Exactly what to deliver (a file, a link, a repository, a video, ...).
- Any required format or size limits.
- Anything forbidden, such as copied content or AI-generated work passed off as entirely your own when the rules say otherwise.

### 2. Plan before you build

Spend ten minutes planning. A rough outline beats an hour of random work:

- What is the one thing your entry should do well?
- What does a good version look like? Find one example to aim at.
- What can you realistically finish before the deadline?

### 3. Build your entry

Work in small steps and save often. If you are using an AI agent to help, give it clear, short instructions and check its output as you go — the entry is yours, so the quality is your responsibility either way.

Keep your work original. Copying someone else's entry, or submitting work you did not make, can get you disqualified.

### 4. Submit before the deadline

Follow the submission steps on the bounty page. Common submission methods include pasting a link, uploading a file, or connecting a repository.

- Submit **early** if you can. Last-minute uploads fail at the worst times: slow internet, a wrong file, a form that needs one more field.
- After submitting, check that your entry actually appears. Open the bounty page and confirm your name is on the list.

### 5. Wait for judging

When the deadline passes, entries are ranked — by judges, by community votes, by an automated score, or by a mix, depending on the bounty. The bounty page explains how ranking works for that competition. Results are usually published on the same page.

## How the Leaderboard Works

The leaderboard is the live ranking of entries for a bounty. A few things worth knowing:

- **Rankings move.** While a bounty is open, new entries arrive and scores change. An early high rank is encouraging, not a guarantee.
- **Only the top places are paid.** Each bounty states how many places share the prize — for example, the top 50. If you finish just outside that range, you still keep the experience (and the work) for next time.
- **Ties and close calls happen.** Small differences in quality can move you several places, so polish matters: fix typos, check links, and make sure images load.
- **Final results are what count.** Provisional or mid-competition ranks are snapshots. The ranking at the close is the one that decides rewards. If your rank drops after a strong start, treat it as a signal: look at what the entries above you did better, and carry that lesson into your next entry instead of chasing the board.

Do not panic if your first entry lands far down the board. Most people who win regularly started with entries that earned nothing — they just kept entering, and each entry taught them something.

## How to Claim Your Reward

If your entry finishes in a paid place, here is what usually happens next:

1. **Check the results.** After the bounty closes, the final leaderboard is published on the bounty page. Confirm your rank and whether it is inside the paid places.
2. **Watch for a notification.** make.fun typically notifies winners through the platform and/or by email. This is why signing up with an email you check matters.
3. **Follow the claim instructions.** Each bounty or reward may have its own claim flow — for example, confirming a payout wallet or address, or clicking a claim button in your account. Follow the instructions exactly as given on the bounty page or in the notification.
4. **Complete the claim promptly.** Rewards can have a claim window. Do not leave it for weeks; claim as soon as you are able.
5. **Keep records.** Save a screenshot of your final rank and any claim confirmation. If anything goes wrong, that record makes support much easier.

Important: never share private keys, seed phrases, or passwords with anyone claiming to help you claim a reward. Official claim flows happen inside make.fun itself. If someone messages you asking for keys, it is a scam.

## Tips for Beginners

These are the lessons most first-timers learn the hard way. Learn them the easy way instead:

1. **Read the rules before you build.** The most common reason a good entry earns nothing is a missed rule, not a lack of talent.
2. **Study the leaderboard first.** Before building anything, spend twenty minutes looking at what already ranks well. Imitate the structure, not the content.
3. **Ship something simple and finished.** A complete, simple entry beats an ambitious, half-finished one every time.
4. **Submit early, improve later.** Get a valid entry in before the deadline, then use the remaining time to polish it if the bounty allows updates.
5. **Check your links and files.** Broken links, missing images, and private repositories are silent killers. Open your submission as a stranger would.
6. **Use AI as a helper, not a replacement.** An AI agent can draft, debug, and brainstorm with you — but you should understand and check everything you submit. Show your entry to a friend before submitting, too: a fresh pair of eyes catches typos and confusing parts in minutes.
7. **Enter more than once.** Your second bounty will be easier than your first, and your fifth easier still. Volume plus reflection is the fastest way to improve.
8. **Have fun with it.** The platform is literally called make.fun. If an entry stops being enjoyable, switch to a bounty that excites you — the work will show it.

## Your First Week on make.fun

If you like plans, here is a simple one-week path from zero to your first submitted entry:

- **Day 1 — Sign up and look around.** Create your account (you can use <https://make.fun/r/zfymqfdt> if this guide brought you here), fill in your profile, and browse five open bounties without committing to any.
- **Day 2 — Study the leaderboard.** Pick the bounty that interests you most and read its top three entries closely. Write down what each one does well and what you would do differently.
- **Day 3 — Sketch your entry.** Write a one-paragraph outline: what you will deliver, how it will be structured, and what "done" looks like. Show the outline to a friend and ask if it makes sense to them.
- **Days 4–5 — Build.** Work in short sessions. Save often, test your links, and check your work as a stranger would. If you use an AI agent, review everything it produces before keeping it.
- **Day 6 — Submit early.** Submit your entry a full day before the deadline if you can, then confirm it appears on the bounty page.
- **Day 7 — Polish and share.** Use the last day to fix typos, improve one weak spot, and — if you liked the experience — invite a friend with your own referral link so they can try a bounty too.

One week, one entry, one real lesson. Repeat that a few times and you will be surprised how fast the leaderboard starts to feel reachable.

## FAQ

**Do I need to pay to join a bounty?**

Many bounties are free to enter. Some may have an entry fee or require a paid step — the bounty page always says so. Read it before you start.

**Do I need to know how to code?**

Not necessarily. Bounties cover many skills: writing, design, video, forecasting, research, and more. Pick a bounty that matches what you can already do, and learn the rest as you go.

**Can I use AI to help with my entry?**

In most cases, yes — AI-assisted work is common on make.fun, and many entries are built with an AI agent. Always follow the specific bounty's rules, and make sure the final entry is genuinely your work and something you understand.

**How do I know if I won?**

Final results appear on the bounty page after it closes, and winners are usually notified by email or on the platform. Check the page and your inbox.
